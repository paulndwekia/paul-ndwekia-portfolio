Financial marketsCompleted · Live

Kenya Financial Analytics

A research terminal for exploring Kenya’s financial markets through official Central Bank of Kenya observations. The product brings currency markets, Treasury-bill auctions, yield curves and practical valuation and risk tools into one connected workspace.

The question: can market observations and practical financial models sit in one place without losing the source and context behind each number?

What I worked on: a Next.js and React application with TypeScript, Python services, Neon PostgreSQL and charting. Its workspaces cover FX analysis, market intelligence, yield curves, Treasury bills, bond pricing, options and Greeks, backtesting, risk analysis and reporting.

Research discipline: historical observations remain distinguishable from calculations. The downloadable workbook documents the CBK source lineage, quote conventions, sampling rules and limitations; missing observations are not silently replaced with zero.

Fixed incomeCompleted · Live

Kenya Yield Curve & Interest Rate Risk Engine

A focused fixed-income research environment for studying the relationship between Treasury yields, maturity and bond valuation. It connects observed Treasury-market information with the financial mathematics used to assess price and interest-rate sensitivity.

The question: how do changing yields and maturity affect the value and risk of a fixed-income instrument?

What it covers: Treasury-bill observations, curve construction, bond cash flows and pricing, yield to maturity, duration, modified duration, convexity and DV01. The project reflects the fixed-income topics I study and apply through financial engineering.

Macroeconomic researchCompleted · Live

GDP Atlas — Global Economic Research

An independent macroeconomic dashboard for exploring the size, growth and distribution of economic activity across countries and regions. The aim is to make comparisons easier while keeping indicator definitions and source coverage visible.

The question: how can someone compare economies without mixing different measures, observation years or reported data with forecasts?

What I worked on: a Next.js, React and TypeScript product with a global overview, country explorer, comparison tools, rankings, regional analysis, a data explorer and CSV downloads. World Bank reported indicators are kept distinct from IMF World Economic Outlook estimates and projections.

Research discipline: the observation year is not presented as the current calendar year by default. Missing values are omitted rather than converted to zero, and units and source methodology remain part of the exploration.

Investment researchLive dashboard · In progress

Kenya Investment Intelligence

A Kenyan market research platform developed alongside the Kenya Investment Outlook 2026 and the Rock Investment Bank Research Challenge 2026. This is collaborative work with Edward Chege Waweru, bringing together equity-market analysis, fixed income, macroeconomic indicators, risk research and data provenance.

Research base: the October 2026 report documents a Rock Data Plus dataset with 181,554 retained NSE observations across 74 securities or series, covering 3 January 2017 to 29 June 2026. Raw source data is not redistributed through this portfolio.

What is still in progress: approved data integration, report validation and automatic deployment from GitHub. The dashboard is a live first release, not a claim that every research module is complete.

Research integrity: earlier security-level outputs contained non-finite values and unstable risk results. The published report withholds those rankings pending validation rather than presenting them as investment signals. Its preliminary recent-market summary is also clearly marked as not yet revalidated.

Financial technologyLive build · In progress

FINTEL — Kenya Financial Intelligence

An institutional-style Kenyan financial intelligence workspace built around official Central Bank of Kenya data, a persistent market-data layer and a separate quantitative engine. Its architecture connects ingestion, validation, storage, calculations and an analytical interface.

What the system includes: official FX, Treasury-bill, Treasury-bond and yield-curve ingestion; source lineage and duplicate handling; a React and TypeScript terminal; Express and tRPC APIs; MySQL/TiDB persistence; and Python models for bond pricing, options and portfolio-risk analysis.

What is not being overstated: CRM and research/news feeds, some macro and cross-market modules, historical curve replay and report export remain unfinished. Portfolio risk requires authorised portfolio positions and aligned history. The product is actively being developed.

One rule across the research

Observed data, derived calculations and forward-looking scenarios are not interchangeable. I aim to show the source, sample period, method and known limitation beside the result. When the evidence is incomplete, the portfolio should say so.

Research approach

Careful with the numbers. Practical with the tools.

The common thread is a workflow that starts with a real question and ends with something another person can inspect, use or challenge.

In financial research
Define the instrument and sample, understand the source, check the assumptions, calculate the measure and state what the result does — and does not — imply. For fixed income, that includes cash flows, discounting, yield, duration, convexity and interest-rate sensitivity.

In software and data products
Build the ingestion and storage path as carefully as the interface. Keep live observations apart from sample values, expose incomplete integrations, make the navigation useful and ensure the product works on a phone as well as a desktop.

In collaboration
Distinguish shared work from individual contribution, document open decisions and keep project status accurate. The Kenya Investment Intelligence research is presented as collaborative work, not as a solo publication.

In public presentation
Use real product screenshots, original photographs and direct links to deliverables. Avoid fabricated market numbers, invented outcomes or visuals that imply a feature exists when it does not.
