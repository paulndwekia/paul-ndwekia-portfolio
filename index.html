How I approach the work

Thoughtful work, from first question to useful product.

I’m interested in the space between a real problem and something that genuinely helps. I try to understand the problem before reaching for a tool, and I keep the people who will use the result in mind as I build.

I don’t think every good idea needs an app, a complicated model or a long list of features. Sometimes the most useful first step is a better question, a cleaner dataset or a clearer explanation of what the numbers are saying.

That way of thinking shapes different kinds of work. In financial research, it means being careful about sources and separating what has happened from what might happen. In software, it means making the interface understandable and checking that it works beyond my own laptop. For a business website, it means helping a real customer quickly understand what the company does and how to get in touch.

Understand the problem

Start with the person and the question.

Who is trying to do what? What is getting in their way? I want to understand what a useful answer would look like before deciding what to build.

Respect the evidence

Let the evidence set the limits.

I check sources, assumptions and gaps rather than making a result look more certain than it is. Observations, calculations and scenarios should be easy to tell apart.

Build for real use

Build, test and keep learning.

I turn the work into something people can navigate, try it on real screens, listen to what feels unclear and improve it. The first version is a place to learn, not a reason to stop.

For me, a good result is not the most complicated thing I can build. It is something that helps someone understand an issue more clearly, save a little time or make a better-informed decision.
